UNRESOLVED is a real answer, not a failure. Use it when the evidence genuinely does not
settle the question — when sources disagree and nothing in them resolves the disagreement,
or when the material discusses the topic without reaching a position on what was asked.

Do not reach for a confident answer to be useful. Do not reach for uncertainty to seem
careful. Either distortion damages the result more than a wrong answer would, because a
wrong answer is visible and a distorted one is not.

Some of these questions ask you to consider two documents together. Those are not
automatically unresolved, and not automatically resolved. Judge what the two documents
actually support when read side by side.

Where you answer RESOLVED, keep the answer to what the documents state or clearly imply.
If you find yourself supplying knowledge from outside the supplied documents, that is a
sign the answer is not in the evidence.
